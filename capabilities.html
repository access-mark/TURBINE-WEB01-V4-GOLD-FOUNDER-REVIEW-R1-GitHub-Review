CAPABILITY LANDSCAPE

Know what needs doing, and what comes next.

A customer gets in touch. Someone makes a promise. Work needs doing and money needs tracking. These are not twelve separate problems. They are parts of the same working day.

THE END IN MIND

A better working day, not another list of features.

Imagine a customer calls while you are out working. You need to remember the request, send a quote, do the job, order a part, invoice and make sure you are paid. Each capability below exists to make that one story easier, not to give you another menu to learn.

THE FIVE BELIEFS

What has to be true to reach that outcome.

  1. 01

    Capture the person and promise

    Know who needs something, what they need, and what you agreed.

  2. 02

    Turn the promise into action

    Put work, people, dates, purchases and stock behind the commitment.

  3. 03

    Close the commercial loop

    An approved job should lead to the right invoice and an explainable payment position.

  4. 04

    Show what deserves attention

    Make risks, delays, margins and unanswered questions visible from the original records.

  5. 05

    Help without taking away control

    Guide people through the process while preserving approvals, security and data portability.

01 / 12  ·  YOUR BUSINESS, CONNECTED

Customers, contacts and CRM

Your customers are not scattered conversations. They are relationships with a next step.

Someone recommends you. A potential customer gets in touch. They want an answer, and you need to remember what you agreed. The value is not another contact field. It is knowing who the person is, what they need and what happens next.

HOW THIS HELPS AT DIFFERENT SCALES
Working alone

A repeat customer, address and next visit are easy to find.

Growing team

The next colleague can see what was agreed and who owns the reply.

QSE / SME

Account ownership and handoffs are visible, with permission boundaries.

Medium / enterprise

Customer history can be linked to approved activity across teams and locations.

The next steps

  1. 01Capture the enquiry or introduction
  2. 02Recognise the person and their history
  3. 03Record what is needed and the next action
  4. 04Link the accepted promise to delivery
  5. 05Keep the commercial and service history available
Explore detailed scope and operating considerations

Maintain a shared view of organisations, people, relationships and commercial activity. Connect customer context to quotations, commitments, service work and subsequent financial activity instead of rebuilding a customer history across multiple applications.

Why it matters

Customer records are the commercial memory of the business. A single customer relationship should retain the organisations, decision-makers, enquiries, commitments and the work that follows, so the next employee does not have to reconstruct the account from email and spreadsheets.

How it helps in practice

A sole proprietor can follow one prospect from referral to invoice. A growing services company can assign account ownership, track open opportunities and connect accepted work to delivery and collections.

How the work connects

Capture the enquiry; qualify the need; record stakeholders and next actions; prepare the commercial response; preserve the acceptance; carry the same customer context into fulfilment, billing and management review.

02 / 12  ·  YOUR BUSINESS, CONNECTED

Sales, quotations and acceptance

A quotation should become a reliable promise, not a document you later cannot find.

Someone asks for a service, a product or a project. You need a price, a clear scope and an approval. If the customer changes their mind, the latest agreed version should stay connected to the work that follows.

HOW THIS HELPS AT DIFFERENT SCALES
Working alone

Create a simple estimate and follow it to acceptance.

Growing team

Keep the sales handoff and customer approval in one view.

QSE / SME

Apply quote authority, margin checks and controlled changes.

Medium / enterprise

Preserve versions and approvals across multiple sales teams.

The next steps

  1. 01Qualify the request
  2. 02Define scope, price and assumptions
  3. 03Review the commercial conditions
  4. 04Record a controlled acceptance
  5. 05Pass the same commitment into the job or order
Explore detailed scope and operating considerations

Prepare structured quotations, preserve version history and support controlled acceptance. Commercial decisions should be traceable to the precise scope and price approved, reducing disputes caused by outdated documents or informal changes.

Why it matters

Sales execution should convert a customer need into a clearly controlled commercial commitment. Scope, price, assumptions, revisions, authorisation and acceptance matter because mistakes made here become delivery disputes and cash-flow problems later.

How it helps in practice

An IT reseller can quote equipment and services together. A consulting firm can define milestones and payment terms. A contractor can document exclusions and change requests before work begins.

How the work connects

Qualify the opportunity; assemble scope and price; review margin and conditions; obtain approval; issue a controlled quotation; record acceptance; hand the commitment to delivery without rekeying it.

03 / 12  ·  YOUR BUSINESS, CONNECTED

Work, projects and scheduling

Know what has to happen, who owns it and whether it is actually finished.

A plumber needs a booking and the right parts. A creative agency needs feedback before design work can continue. A medium business needs a clear chain of responsibility. Work is not completed because the task was moved to a different list.

HOW THIS HELPS AT DIFFERENT SCALES
Working alone

See the next appointment and what is due.

Growing team

Hand tasks to the right person without losing the customer.

QSE / SME

Coordinate people, dates, procurement and milestones.

Medium / enterprise

Provide cross-team delivery visibility and auditable authority.

The next steps

  1. 01Turn the promise into assigned work
  2. 02Plan people, dates and dependencies
  3. 03Record progress and exceptions
  4. 04Confirm completion and evidence
  5. 05Carry the result into billing and reporting
Explore detailed scope and operating considerations

Coordinate tasks, appointments, resources and delivery obligations. Keep customer commitments visible alongside the operational work required to fulfil them, with clear ownership and status.

People and teams are part of the intended operating model: staff assignments, responsibilities, availability and workload should connect to business work and reporting. Employee records, leave, payroll and statutory HR processing require separately qualified capability contracts; this preview does not claim those functions are live.

Why it matters

Accepted commitments must translate into accountable work. Teams need to know what is due, who owns it, which dependencies are unresolved and what evidence establishes completion. Operational work and project work should be visible in the same business context.

How it helps in practice

A freelancer tracks deliverables and client approvals. A service company assigns technicians and visits. A manufacturer coordinates customer orders with production milestones and exceptions.

How the work connects

Open work from the accepted commitment; allocate owners and dates; track dependencies, changes and evidence; resolve exceptions; confirm completion; trigger the appropriate billing and reporting steps.

04 / 12  ·  YOUR BUSINESS, CONNECTED

Procurement and supplier operations

Buy what the business actually needs, with the right authority and cost context.

A service business needs parts for a job. A contractor needs materials on site. A distributor needs stock. Each purchase is a cash commitment, often caused by customer work, and should be visible before it becomes an unexpected bill.

HOW THIS HELPS AT DIFFERENT SCALES
Working alone

Note supplies required for the next job.

Growing team

Order against approved work and delivery dates.

QSE / SME

Route purchase requests, budgets and supplier approvals.

Medium / enterprise

Track purchasing commitments across teams and entities where qualified.

The next steps

  1. 01Recognise the need
  2. 02Compare supply, price and availability
  3. 03Obtain the applicable approval
  4. 04Receive and verify what arrived
  5. 05Connect costs and supplier obligations to the work
Explore detailed scope and operating considerations

Follow purchasing from demand and approval to order, goods receipt and supplier processing. Distinguish what was requested, what was delivered and what remains financially unresolved.

Why it matters

Purchasing is not an isolated administrative task: it is a commitment of cash, often driven by a customer order, project, service requirement or stock threshold. Supplier selection, approval and receipt must remain connected to the underlying need.

How it helps in practice

An agency buys specialist subcontractor capacity. An installer orders components against an accepted job. A distributor replenishes stock while protecting available cash.

How the work connects

Identify the requirement; evaluate supplier terms; approve the purchase; track the order and receipt; reconcile supplier obligations; allocate costs to the relevant work, stock or operating expense.

05 / 12  ·  YOUR BUSINESS, CONNECTED

Inventory, stock and cost

Promise stock you actually have, and understand what it really costs.

A retailer cannot fulfil a sale from an empty shelf. An installer cannot finish when a part was never ordered. Visibility is about more than quantities: it is the promise to the customer and the cash tied up in stock.

HOW THIS HELPS AT DIFFERENT SCALES
Working alone

Track the parts consumed on a job.

Growing team

See stock held for customers and incoming orders.

QSE / SME

Plan replenishment, reservation and valuation.

Medium / enterprise

Review inventory, location and working-capital exposure.

The next steps

  1. 01Know the item and its location
  2. 02Capture receipts and quantities
  3. 03Reserve or allocate stock to work
  4. 04Record issues, use or returns
  5. 05Review costs, shortages and replenishment
Explore detailed scope and operating considerations

Understand stock movements, availability, purchasing and cost-of-goods-sold relationships. For product-led businesses, inventory decisions should connect to both fulfilment and financial context.

Why it matters

Stock records need to explain what the business owns, where it is, what is committed to customers and what it costs. Inventory affects purchasing, delivery promises, margin and working capital.

How it helps in practice

A retailer manages replenishment and availability. An IT reseller tracks devices allocated to orders. A maintenance provider accounts for parts consumed during a customer job.

How the work connects

Record items and locations; receive and value stock; reserve quantities for commitments; issue or consume items; handle adjustments and returns; reflect stock movement in operational and financial visibility.

Finance, billing and receivables

Know which work is billable, what is still owed and what needs following up.

A solo service provider should not spend Sunday reconciling a notebook and bank messages. A QSE financial manager needs the invoice to relate to accepted work. Finance should not begin by recreating the business event.

HOW THIS HELPS AT DIFFERENT SCALES
Working alone

Find unpaid invoices alongside upcoming appointments.

Growing team

Bill the right milestone or delivered job.

QSE / SME

See credit control and operating cash exposure.

Medium / enterprise

Require auditable treatment and qualified financial policies.

The next steps

  1. 01Confirm an approved billable event
  2. 02Prepare and authorise the invoice
  3. 03Record receipts and allocations
  4. 04Investigate disputes and overdue items
  5. 05Review the outstanding money against underlying work
Explore detailed scope and operating considerations

Connect invoicing, customer balances, receipts and relevant accounting controls to the underlying work. The objective is better financial visibility with preserved transaction history, not a disconnected finance screen.

Why it matters

Financial activity should follow the real commercial event, not begin as a disconnected second process. Billing, receipts, outstanding balances and operating costs need traceability to customers, work and approvals.

How it helps in practice

A sole proprietor sees unpaid invoices alongside upcoming commitments. A professional-services firm bills milestones. A growing SME reviews receivables, supplier obligations and cash exposure.

How the work connects

Confirm billable events; create and approve invoices; issue statements where appropriate; record receipts and allocations; manage exceptions and overdue accounts; review revenue and cash position.

07 / 12  ·  YOUR BUSINESS, CONNECTED

Banking interfaces and financial controls

A bank entry should have a business explanation, not just a date and amount.

A payment lands in the account with an unclear reference. Is it a deposit, a part-payment or a duplicate? A useful financial system should help an authorised person connect money to the transaction and deal visibly with exceptions.

HOW THIS HELPS AT DIFFERENT SCALES
Working alone

See which customers have paid and which have not.

Growing team

Reduce time spent matching and chasing receipts.

QSE / SME

Protect approval rights and reconciliation evidence.

Medium / enterprise

Control banking interfaces, records and reconciled positions where qualified.

The next steps

  1. 01Receive permitted statement or transaction data
  2. 02Match to known invoices or obligations
  3. 03Flag items that do not reconcile
  4. 04Approve supported corrections
  5. 05Keep an auditable financial picture
Explore detailed scope and operating considerations

TURBINE is intended to be the primary financial operating environment. Where external banking, payment or statutory interfaces are essential, they require consent, reconciliation and independent qualification. Connecting third-party business applications is not the product proposition.

Why it matters

The bank is an external financial infrastructure dependency, not a competing business application. TURBINE must preserve controlled financial records, authorisations, reconciliation evidence and accountability around the movement of money.

How it helps in practice

An owner reconciles customer receipts against invoices. A finance team investigates unmatched transactions, protects payment approval authority and maintains an auditable record of adjustments.

How the work connects

Import or receive permitted transaction information; match receipts and payments; investigate exceptions; approve corrections; preserve supporting evidence; review reconciled balances and outstanding items.

Reporting and management visibility

A report should tell you what to do, not just display a chart.

The owner wants to know which job to finish before lunch. The manager wants to know which project is going late. The executive wants to know where margins or collections have changed. Those answers should come from the same business records.

HOW THIS HELPS AT DIFFERENT SCALES
Working alone

See today’s urgent jobs and unpaid items.

Growing team

Review team workload, deadlines and exceptions.

QSE / SME

Connect revenue, cost, cash and operational performance.

Medium / enterprise

Trace aggregate results back to their accountable records.

The next steps

  1. 01Agree the business question
  2. 02Read from governed source records
  3. 03Surface exceptions and changes
  4. 04Explain the reasons and owners
  5. 05Take an authorised next action
Explore detailed scope and operating considerations

Bring operational and financial information into meaningful management views. A useful report should explain which records drive a result, where attention is needed and which figures remain uncertain.

Why it matters

Management information must explain the state of the whole business rather than present isolated dashboards. Leaders need to connect sales demand, delivery capacity, costs, receivables, commitments and exceptions.

How it helps in practice

A sole proprietor reviews overdue work and collections. A sales manager sees quotations awaiting approval. An operations leader sees blocked work and supplier dependencies.

How the work connects

Collect authoritative operational events; aggregate by customer, owner, period and business activity; surface exceptions; investigate underlying records; take action; monitor whether the action resolved the problem.

Turbine Guide and contextual intelligence

You should not need to be a software expert to do your job.

When the gardener is trying to send the first quote, the answer should be a clear next step. When an approver opens a high-value purchase, guidance should explain the decision and its evidence without bypassing controls.

HOW THIS HELPS AT DIFFERENT SCALES
Working alone

Start with familiar language and a small set of actions.

Growing team

Find the right task without knowing all the menus.

QSE / SME

Explain workflows and approval boundaries as the team grows.

Medium / enterprise

Preserve role-based context, governance and explainability.

The next steps

  1. 01Recognise who is working and why
  2. 02Show the context that matters now
  3. 03Explain the next allowed action
  4. 04Help resolve exceptions without guessing
  5. 05Keep the original record and authority visible
Explore detailed scope and operating considerations

Help people find the right screen, understand a workflow and identify the next relevant action. Predictive and generative features require separate verification before being offered as production capabilities.

Why it matters

Guidance belongs inside the work. Users should understand what a task means, why it matters, which information is required and what happens next without needing a separate training manual for every workflow.

How it helps in practice

A new employee receives help completing an enquiry. An owner sees why an invoice cannot be finalised. A manager is directed to the record requiring approval.

How the work connects

Identify the user context and intended outcome; explain required information; guide the next authorised action; highlight missing evidence; preserve human review for consequential decisions.

DataPort Import / Export Studio

Your business history should be portable, checked and yours to take away.

A business with five spreadsheets should not have to start from an empty customer list. A QSE needs mappings, error reports, validation and reconciliation. DataPort is designed as a governed migration and export journey, not a blind bulk upload.

HOW THIS HELPS AT DIFFERENT SCALES
Working alone

Bring names, contacts and outstanding work safely.

Growing team

Import data with templates and clear error messages.

QSE / SME

Reconcile records and financial totals before cutover.

Medium / enterprise

Handle high-volume mapped transfers with controlled retries and export.

The next steps

  1. 01Choose the source records and template
  2. 02Map fields and validate their meaning
  3. 03Review errors before committing
  4. 04Reconcile counts and important balances
  5. 05Retain an authorised export route
Explore detailed scope and operating considerations

A governed approach to importing and exporting business records at scale: templates, field mapping, validation, error handling and portability. Customer, contact, opportunity, accounting and transaction migrations require mapping and reconciliation before production cutover.

Why it matters

A full-business adoption depends on trustworthy migration. DataPort is designed to move business records into TURBINE with explicit mapping, validation, error reporting and reconciliation, and to support portability when authorised. It is not a strategy for indefinitely operating disconnected software.

How it helps in practice

A consultant migrates contacts, outstanding invoices and supplier records. A distributor migrates product masters, stock balances and customer accounts after validation and cutover planning.

How the work connects

Inventory source data; define templates and mappings; run trial imports; validate duplicates and relationships; resolve errors; reconcile totals; approve cutover; retain export and recovery evidence.

Communication and collaboration

One customer promise should not disappear between messages and meetings.

A client asks a question by email; the job is assigned on a call; the approval arrives later. TURBINE’s intended communication context is about retaining the relevant decision and responsibility instead of relying on someone’s inbox as the company memory.

HOW THIS HELPS AT DIFFERENT SCALES
Working alone

Find the last instruction and promised follow-up.

Growing team

Share useful notes and changes with the team.

QSE / SME

Connect communication to controlled customer and work records.

Medium / enterprise

Preserve appropriate accountability and data boundaries.

The next steps

  1. 01Capture the relevant communication
  2. 02Associate it with the right person or work
  3. 03Confirm the decision and next action
  4. 04Notify or route through approved channels
  5. 05Keep the history intelligible and access-controlled
Explore detailed scope and operating considerations

Keep important conversations, decisions and handoffs associated with the relevant business records. The intended model is contextual communication, not another isolated inbox.

Why it matters

Communication should remain attached to the business record and the responsibility it creates. Teams need to distinguish discussion from commitments, approvals, customer instructions and evidence of delivery.

How it helps in practice

An agency records client feedback against a deliverable. A service team links a customer instruction to a job. A manager reviews an approval request with the underlying quotation visible.

How the work connects

Capture relevant correspondence or notes; associate them with the customer and work; assign actions; record decisions; notify responsible users; preserve the business history.

12 / 12  ·  YOUR BUSINESS, CONNECTED

Access, permissions and audit

Make it easy to work, and difficult to act outside your responsibility.

A technician should see the job they need to complete. A finance approver should see the authorised spend and supporting evidence. A director needs visibility without giving everybody the same powers.

HOW THIS HELPS AT DIFFERENT SCALES
Working alone

Keep personal access simple and relevant.

Growing team

Know who is responsible for each step.

QSE / SME

Apply financial and operational approval boundaries.

Medium / enterprise

Prove organisation isolation, audit and recovery before production.

The next steps

  1. 01Identify the authorised person
  2. 02Establish role and organisation scope
  3. 03Check authority before sensitive action
  4. 04Record decisions and significant changes
  5. 05Demonstrate audit and recovery evidence
Explore detailed scope and operating considerations

Businesses need controlled user access, tenant separation, appropriate permissions and traceable changes. Staff accounts, delegated authority and accountable actions must follow the same governance model. These controls are qualification requirements, not assumptions based on an attractive interface.

Why it matters

A holistic operating environment requires strong boundaries. People should see and perform only what their roles authorise, and important commercial and financial changes should be attributable and reviewable.

How it helps in practice

An owner delegates quoting without granting unrestricted financial authority. A branch manager sees appropriate operational records. Finance retains control of sensitive adjustments and approvals.

How the work connects

Establish identity and roles; define permitted actions; enforce separation of duties; record consequential changes; review exceptions; recover access through governed procedures.
