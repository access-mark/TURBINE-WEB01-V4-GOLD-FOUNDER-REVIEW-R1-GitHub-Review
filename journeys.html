CONNECTED OPERATING JOURNEYS

Follow the business from beginning to end.

These six examples show how customers, work, purchasing, inventory and money should move through one coherent business environment. Each is an intended journey, subject to validated product capabilities.

THE END IN MIND

See one customer request travel through every important handoff to a clear, accountable result.

Someone calls for a garden clean-up. The job is priced, accepted, scheduled, completed, invoiced and paid. A distributor’s order or a corporate service contract takes more steps, but the essential discipline remains: never lose the thread.

THE FIVE BELIEFS

What has to be true to reach that outcome.

  1. 01

    Begin with a real trigger

    A customer request, stock shortage or project instruction starts the journey.

  2. 02

    Make the promise explicit

    Scope, timing, price, owners and approvals have to be understood.

  3. 03

    Follow every handoff

    Suppliers, technicians, project staff and finance all work from the same context.

  4. 04

    Know what complete means

    Evidence of delivery, acceptance, exception or follow-up ends the operational uncertainty.

  5. 05

    Close the financial loop

    Only then can the business understand billing, payment, cost and performance.

01 / 06

From enquiry to reconciled payment

A new customer request should not become six disconnected pieces of administration. The target journey connects commercial intent, agreed scope, delivery and the financial record so an authorised team can understand what happened and what remains outstanding.

  1. 01Record a customer and contact
  2. 02Capture the enquiry and opportunity
  3. 03Prepare a controlled quotation
  4. 04Review and record acceptance
  5. 05Create and assign delivery work
  6. 06Capture completion and exceptions
  7. 07Issue the corresponding invoice
  8. 08Match the receipt and review outstanding balances

Business outcomeOne customer context across sales, work and finance, with fewer manual handovers and a clearer path to cash.

Control and qualification boundaryQuotation version, approval authority, tax treatment, invoice linkage and bank reconciliation require qualified operating contracts.

02 / 06

From demand to stock to fulfilment

For product-led businesses, a sale relies on a dependable chain of purchasing, availability and cost information. TURBINE is designed to relate procurement decisions to the physical movement of goods and the commercial transaction.

  1. 01Identify demand and available stock
  2. 02Request or approve purchasing
  3. 03Issue a supplier purchase order
  4. 04Receive and verify delivered quantities
  5. 05Record stock movement and cost
  6. 06Allocate or reserve stock for fulfilment
  7. 07Complete delivery and customer billing
  8. 08Reconcile supplier and customer financial records

Business outcomePurchasing, inventory, fulfilment and financial exposure become part of one traceable journey.

Control and qualification boundaryStock valuation, supplier invoices, tax, segregation of duties and any statutory or ERP integration remain subject to qualification.

03 / 06

From service request to confirmed delivery

A service business needs visibility across commitments, people and outcomes. The intended workflow connects the client relationship, scheduling, work execution, exception handling and the resulting commercial obligation.

  1. 01Capture the client request
  2. 02Check customer and service context
  3. 03Agree scope or authorise a job
  4. 04Assign an appropriate resource
  5. 05Schedule and carry out work
  6. 06Record evidence, time and exceptions
  7. 07Confirm completion or remedial action
  8. 08Bill and review service performance

Business outcomeBetter accountability from a customer promise through to completed, billable work.

Control and qualification boundaryMobile/offline behavior, field signatures, specialist service workflows and automatic dispatch must be individually demonstrated before they are promised.

04 / 06

From project proposal to margin visibility

Professional and project-led organisations need one view of the approved proposal, the work delivered, costs incurred and what can be invoiced. The intended model avoids separating commercial context from project reality.

  1. 01Qualify an opportunity
  2. 02Prepare scoped proposal and estimate
  3. 03Record customer acceptance
  4. 04Establish milestones and accountable owners
  5. 05Capture deliverables, effort and costs
  6. 06Review deviations and change requests
  7. 07Approve billing or progress claims
  8. 08Assess revenue, obligations and project results

Business outcomeOperational teams and commercial leaders can work against the same approved scope and evidence.

Control and qualification boundaryTimesheets, revenue recognition, tax, multi-currency and complex project accounting depend on explicit product qualification.

05 / 06

From supplier commitment to payment control

Supplier spend must remain understandable from the original requirement through approval, receipt and financial settlement. The BusinessOS design aligns operational procurement evidence with finance instead of relying on disconnected spreadsheets.

  1. 01Register supplier and required item
  2. 02Record purchase request
  3. 03Apply approval limits and policy
  4. 04Issue a recorded purchase order
  5. 05Verify goods or service receipt
  6. 06Reconcile the supplier invoice
  7. 07Authorise settlement and link banking evidence
  8. 08Review spend, exposure and outstanding items

Business outcomeMore disciplined spending, fewer unmatched obligations and clearer management visibility.

Control and qualification boundaryPayment execution, banking connections, delegated authority and accounting rules must pass financial and security gates.

06 / 06

From scattered systems to one business environment

Adoption is an operating change, not a bulk file upload. The intended process evaluates existing workflows, business data and governance before an organisation moves its operating activity into TURBINE.

  1. 01Discover the present operating model
  2. 02Agree scope, roles and acceptance criteria
  3. 03Inventory and map existing records
  4. 04Validate and reconcile representative imports
  5. 05Configure permissions and workspaces
  6. 06Test real end-to-end use cases safely
  7. 07Approve controlled cutover when qualified
  8. 08Operate, reconcile and preserve export options

Business outcomeA clearer migration path with explicit acceptance rather than an unverified big-bang implementation.

Control and qualification boundaryProduction trials, live cutover and connector use are subject to TURBINE Command provisioning, tenant isolation and independent release qualification.

How to read these journeys

These are intended operating patterns and qualification scenarios, not claims that every workflow is live or certified. The actual product, commercial terms, data model, connectors and controls must be demonstrated against the applicable release gates.
